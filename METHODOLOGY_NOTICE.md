# JESI Methodology and Research Documentation Notice

The JAS Unified Economic Strength Index (JESI) is an original research framework developed by JAS.

This notice clarifies the distinction between the repository's software license and the JESI research methodology.

## 1. Scope

This notice applies particularly to the JESI conceptual and methodological materials contained in:

- `methodology/`
- `docs/methodology/`
- methodological sections of `README.md`
- related research documentation and specifications

These materials describe the conceptual structure, pillar architecture, indicator selection, normalization procedures, weighting framework, aggregation approach, robustness procedures, validation strategy and related research design of JESI.

## 2. Relationship to the MIT License

The repository contains source code released under the MIT License.

The presence of the MIT License does not mean that every research concept, methodological document, dataset or third-party material in the repository is automatically MIT-licensed.

The repository should therefore be understood as containing distinct categories of material:

## 4. Research Framework

The JESI framework is designed as a multidimensional analytical approach to economic strength.

Its research architecture includes the following broad pillars:

- Growth
- Productivity
- Connectivity
- Resilience
- Strategic Autonomy

The methodology also includes procedures for indicator construction, normalization, weighting, aggregation, robustness testing and empirical validation.

These elements constitute part of the JAS research framework and should be attributed accordingly when directly used or substantially adapted.

## 7. Research Status

JESI is a proposed research framework.

It should not be represented as an established official international
economic index or as an index endorsed by any external statistical
institution.

The current Master Version 1.0 empirical validation program evaluates
the documented production specification using:

- indicator redundancy/correlation analysis;
- normalization sensitivity;
- weight sensitivity;
- aggregation sensitivity;
- historical validation;
- missing-data coverage analysis;
- balanced-panel sensitivity;
- final methodological audit.

The production calculation uses complete-case country-year observations.

No missing observation is silently imputed, interpolated, fabricated,
or automatically replaced.

The current theoretical panel contains 40 country-years for five
countries over 2016–2023.

The current production complete-case sample contains 34 observations.

Country-level coverage is unequal. Bangladesh currently contributes
2 complete observations, while India, Vietnam, Indonesia and Malaysia
each contribute 8 complete observations.

This unequal coverage is explicitly treated as a methodological
limitation and is evaluated through a separate balanced-panel sensitivity
analysis.

The balanced-panel sensitivity analysis does not replace the production
result and does not modify the baseline JESI methodology.

Completion of the empirical validation program represents a methodological
milestone, not universal scientific validation.
